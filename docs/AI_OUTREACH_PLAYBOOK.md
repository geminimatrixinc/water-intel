# AI-Assisted Outreach Playbook — Mike's Guide

> **Why this exists:** Mike is a strong builder but self-identifies as not a natural salesperson.
> AI (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) can close that gap by handling the writing, tone, and follow-up — while Mike stays in control of the relationships and decisions.
> This playbook gives step-by-step instructions to copy-paste into any AI chat when it's time to do outreach, grant writing, or relationship management.

---

## Golden Rule

**You are not selling.** You're an Indigenous developer who built something real for a problem you care about, and you're asking people if it's useful. That's community engagement, not sales. AI just helps you say it clearly.

---

## Step 1: First Outreach Email

When you're ready to contact someone (FNHA, a tribal council, ISC, a university, etc.), open a new AI chat and paste this:

```
I need help writing an outreach email. Here's the context:

WHO I AM: Mike, Indigenous owner of Gemini Matrix Consulting. I'm a developer, not a salesperson.

WHAT I BUILT: Water-Intel — an AI-powered early-warning platform for water quality.
It uses Ontario Provincial Water Quality Monitoring Network data from the Grand
River watershed — 8 monitoring stations within 45 km of Six Nations, analyzing
5 years of historical trends (2019–2024) across 103 water quality parameters.
Machine learning anomaly detection produces risk scores and alerts.
I have a working prototype with real data from our actual watershed.

WHAT I WANT: I'm looking for feedback, pilot partnerships, or letters of support —
not trying to sell anything right now.

WHO I'M EMAILING: [describe the person/org — e.g., "FNHA Innovation Fund coordinator",
"Band council water department at X Nation", "Professor doing water research at UBC"]

WHAT I WANT TO SAY (in my own words): [write it rough, in whatever way feels natural —
e.g., "I built this thing, I think it could help, can I show you a quick demo?"]

Please write a professional but warm email. Keep it under 200 words.
Don't make it sound corporate or pushy. I want to come across as genuine
and community-minded. Include a line about being open to feedback even if
they're not the right contact.
```

### Tips
- Always write your rough version first — AI polishes, it doesn't replace your voice
- Ask AI to "make it shorter" if the draft feels too long
- Ask AI to "make it warmer" or "less formal" if it sounds stiff

---

## Step 2: Handling Replies

When someone replies to your email, paste this into AI:

```
Someone replied to my outreach email about Water-Intel. Here's what they said:

[paste their reply]

Context: I'm Mike from Gemini Matrix Consulting. I built a water quality
early-warning prototype. I was reaching out for feedback / pilot interest /
letter of support.

Help me draft a reply that:
- Thanks them for responding
- Answers their question directly
- Suggests a next step (demo call, more info, introduction to someone else)
- Keeps the tone warm and genuine, not corporate
- Is under 150 words
```

### Tips
- If they say "not the right person," ask AI to draft a "thanks, could you point me to who is?" reply
- If they say "tell me more," ask AI to draft a 1-paragraph summary with a link to your site/demo
- If they go silent after 7–10 days, use Step 5 (follow-up)

## Step 3: Grant Application Writing

When you find a grant to apply for, paste this:

```
I need help writing a grant application. Here's the context:

PROGRAM: [name of the grant — e.g., "FNHA Innovation Fund"]
AMOUNT REQUESTED: [e.g., "$75,000"]
DEADLINE: [if known]

MY PROJECT: Water-Intel — AI-powered water quality early-warning platform.
Phase 1 (complete): Working prototype using public ECCC data, ML anomaly detection,
risk scoring dashboard.
Phase 2 (what funding is for): Integration with real potable water system data,
pilot deployment in 2 First Nations communities, OCAP®-compliant data governance.

MY COMPANY: Gemini Matrix Consulting, Indigenous-owned tech consulting.

KEY SELLING POINTS:
- Indigenous-led
- Working prototype already built
- OCAP® principles built into the design
- Measurable outcomes (advisory days prevented, operator confidence)

The grant application asks these questions:
[paste the application questions or criteria]

Please draft answers for each question. Use clear, confident language.
Emphasize Indigenous leadership, data sovereignty, and practical outcomes.
Keep each answer focused and under 300 words unless they specify otherwise.
```

### Tips
- Feed AI your existing docs for better output: "Here's my business plan for context: [paste from BUSINESS_PLAN.md]"
- Ask AI to "adjust the tone for a technical audience" (Mitacs) vs. "community impact audience" (FNHA)
- Always review the numbers — AI may hallucinate stats

## Step 4: Meeting Preparation

Before a call or meeting, paste this:

```
I have a meeting about Water-Intel. Help me prepare.

WHO: [e.g., "Water coordinator at Okanagan Indian Band"]
WHAT THEY KNOW: [e.g., "I sent them a cold email, they replied saying they're interested"]
FORMAT: [e.g., "30-minute video call" or "in-person coffee meeting"]

MY PROJECT: Water-Intel — AI water quality early-warning tool. Working prototype
with public ECCC data. Looking for pilot partners for Phase 2 with real water system data.

I'm not a natural salesperson. Please give me:
1. A 60-second opening pitch I can practice (plain language, not jargon)
2. The 5 most likely questions they'll ask and how I should answer
3. What I should ask THEM (to show I'm listening, not just pitching)
4. How to end the meeting with a clear next step
5. Any red flags to watch for
```

### Tips
- Practice the 60-second pitch out loud 2–3 times before the meeting
- Bring your laptop with the dashboard open — showing beats telling
- It's OK to say "I don't know, but I can find out" — that builds trust

---

## Step 5: Follow-Up Emails

If someone hasn't replied in 7–10 days:

```
I sent an outreach email about Water-Intel [7/10/14] days ago and haven't
heard back. Help me write a gentle follow-up.

Original context: [1 sentence about who they are and what you asked]

Please write a short follow-up (under 100 words) that:
- Is friendly, not pushy
- References the original email briefly
- Gives them an easy out ("If this isn't the right time, no worries")
- Reiterates one simple next step
```

### Tips
- One follow-up is fine. Two is OK if spaced 2 weeks apart. Three is too many.
- If no reply after 2 follow-ups, move on — not every door opens
- A "no" is better than silence — it frees you to focus elsewhere

---

## Step 6: Thank You / Post-Meeting

After any meeting or positive interaction:

```
I just had a meeting with [person/org] about Water-Intel. It went [well/okay/mixed].
Here's what happened: [brief summary]

Please draft a thank-you email that:
- Thanks them for their time
- Recaps 1–2 key points from the conversation
- Confirms the next step we agreed on
- Is warm and genuine, under 150 words
```

### Tips
- Send thank-you emails within 24 hours — it makes you memorable
- If they offered to introduce you to someone, reference that specifically
- If they mentioned a concern, acknowledge it: "I appreciated your question about data governance — I'll send more detail on our OCAP® approach"

## Mindset Reminders

- **You built this.** Most people only talk about ideas. You have a working prototype.
- **Asking for feedback is not selling.** It's the opposite — it's humility.
- **One conversation is enough to start.** You don't need 50 contacts. You need 1 who cares.
- **AI handles the words. You bring the authenticity.** That's a team.
- **Every "no" is practice.** And every "not right now" might become "yes" later.
